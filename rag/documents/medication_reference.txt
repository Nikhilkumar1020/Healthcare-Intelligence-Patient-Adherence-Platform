Common Chronic Disease Medications — Adherence Considerations

PURPOSE
=======================================================================

This reference guide summarizes adherence-related considerations for
common medication classes used in chronic disease management programs.
It is designed to help patient support representatives understand
medication-specific adherence challenges.

=======================================================================
SECTION 1: ANTIDIABETIC MEDICATIONS
=======================================================================

METFORMIN (Biguanide)
Primary Use: Type 2 Diabetes Management
Dosage Forms: 500mg, 1000mg tablets; extended-release available
Refill Pattern: Monthly or 90-day supply

Adherence Considerations:
  - GI side effects (nausea, diarrhea) are common in the first 2 weeks
    and are the most frequent cause of early discontinuation
  - Extended-release formulation reduces GI side effects by ~40%
  - Low out-of-pocket cost (generic widely available) improves adherence
  - Patients often stop when they perceive blood sugar is "controlled"
  - Education on long-term benefits of continued use is critical

Common Adherence Barriers:
  - Side effect intolerance (especially in first month)
  - Belief that medication is no longer needed when symptoms improve
  - Prescription changes (dose adjustments) causing confusion

GLIPIZIDE (Sulfonylurea)
Primary Use: Type 2 Diabetes (insulin secretagogue)
Refill Pattern: Monthly

Adherence Considerations:
  - Hypoglycemia risk if dose missed and then doubled
  - Patients should be educated on proper refill timing
  - Adherence declines if hypoglycemic episodes occur

=======================================================================
SECTION 2: ANTIHYPERTENSIVE MEDICATIONS
=======================================================================

LISINOPRIL (ACE Inhibitor)
Primary Use: Hypertension, Heart Failure, Post-MI
Refill Pattern: Monthly or 90-day supply

Adherence Considerations:
  - Dry cough (10–15% of patients) is the most common cause
    of discontinuation; patients should be counseled on alternatives
  - Asymptomatic nature of hypertension is a major barrier
    (patients feel well and see no immediate benefit from continued use)
  - Generic availability improves cost-related adherence

AMLODIPINE (Calcium Channel Blocker)
Primary Use: Hypertension, Angina
Refill Pattern: Monthly

Adherence Considerations:
  - Very well tolerated; peripheral edema may occur
  - Long half-life (30-50 hours) provides some buffer for missed doses
  - Lower adherence risk compared to twice-daily medications

LOSARTAN (ARB)
Primary Use: Hypertension, Diabetic Nephropathy
Refill Pattern: Monthly

Adherence Considerations:
  - No cough (preferred over ACE inhibitors for cough-intolerant patients)
  - Adherence patterns similar to other once-daily antihypertensives
  - Education on asymptomatic benefit is key

=======================================================================
SECTION 3: LIPID-LOWERING MEDICATIONS
=======================================================================

ATORVASTATIN / ROSUVASTATIN (HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors / Statins)
Primary Use: Hyperlipidemia, Cardiovascular Risk Reduction
Refill Pattern: Monthly or 90-day supply

Adherence Considerations:
  - Myalgia (muscle pain) is the most common side effect causing
    discontinuation (reported in 5–10% of patients)
  - Patients must understand that statins are long-term medications
    and that stopping increases cardiovascular risk
  - Annual lipid panels help reinforce medication benefit
  - 90-day supply dispensing significantly improves statin PDC
  - Lower adherence in patients without prior cardiovascular events
    (primary vs. secondary prevention gap)

=======================================================================
SECTION 4: CARDIOVASCULAR MEDICATIONS
=======================================================================

METOPROLOL (Beta-Blocker)
Primary Use: Hypertension, Heart Failure, Post-MI, Arrhythmia
Refill Pattern: Monthly (twice-daily dosing)

Adherence Considerations:
  - Fatigue and bradycardia may cause early discontinuation
  - Abrupt discontinuation is clinically dangerous (rebound hypertension)
  - Patient education on dangers of sudden stopping is critical
  - Once-daily extended-release formulation (metoprolol succinate)
    has better adherence than twice-daily tartrate formulation

WARFARIN (Anticoagulant)
Primary Use: Atrial Fibrillation, DVT/PE prevention
Refill Pattern: Monthly (requires INR monitoring)

Adherence Considerations:
  - High-risk medication; missed doses or irregular adherence
    can result in stroke or bleeding events
  - INR monitoring requirement creates touch points for adherence support
  - Complex dietary and drug interactions require ongoing education
  - DOACs (newer anticoagulants) have replaced warfarin in many cases
    due to better adherence profiles and fewer monitoring requirements

=======================================================================
SECTION 5: RESPIRATORY MEDICATIONS
=======================================================================

ALBUTEROL (Short-Acting Beta-2 Agonist / SABA)
Primary Use: Asthma, COPD (rescue inhaler)
Refill Pattern: As needed; monitoring for overuse is important

Adherence Considerations:
  - Overuse (> 2 rescue inhalers/month) is a red flag for
    inadequate disease control requiring controller therapy review
  - Under-use may reflect avoidance due to cost or access barriers
  - Proper inhaler technique affects medication delivery

FLUTICASONE (Inhaled Corticosteroid / ICS)
Primary Use: Asthma, COPD controller therapy
Refill Pattern: Monthly

Adherence Considerations:
  - Asymptomatic patients often stop ICS therapy prematurely
  - Oral thrush is a manageable side effect that can cause discontinuation
    (rinsing mouth after use prevents this)
  - 90-day supply and adherence programs significantly improve ICS PDC

=======================================================================
SECTION 6: ANTIDEPRESSANTS
=======================================================================

SERTRALINE / ESCITALOPRAM (SSRIs)
Primary Use: Major Depressive Disorder, Anxiety Disorders
Refill Pattern: Monthly

Adherence Considerations:
  - Delayed onset (2–4 weeks to full effect) is a major barrier;
    patients may discontinue early believing the medication does not work
  - Initial side effects (nausea, insomnia) often resolve within 2 weeks
  - Stigma around mental health treatment can affect refill behavior
  - Patients should be counseled never to abruptly discontinue
  - Relapse risk is highest in the first 6 months of treatment
